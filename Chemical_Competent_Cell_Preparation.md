# Preparation of Chemically Competent *E. coli* BL21 (DE3) Cells

---

## Day 1 – Overnight Culture and Reagents

### Equipment

- 37°C incubator (for plates)
- 37°C shaking incubator (for liquid culture)
- Sterile inoculating loops or pipette tips
- 15 mL sterile culture tubes
- 50 mL sterile Falcon tubes
- 500 mL Duran bottles
- 200 mL Duran bottles
- 500 mL culture flasks

### Reagents

- *E. coli* BL21 (DE3) stock (glycerol stock or frozen stock)
- LB agar plates (no antibiotics)
- LB agar + Kanamycin plates
- LB agar + Carbenicillin plates (or other commonly used antibiotic plates)
- LB broth (no antibiotics)
- 0.1 M CaCl₂
- Distilled H₂O
- Glycerol (100%)

### Procedure

(1) Streak plates to check stock quality  
Using a sterile loop or tip, swab *E. coli* BL21 from the frozen glycerol stock. Be sure not to contaminate the stock. Inoculate 5 mL LB broth (no antibiotics) in a sterile culture tube. Using a fresh sterile loop or tip, swab *E. coli* BL21 from the frozen glycerol stock again. Streak onto three plates: LB agar (no antibiotics), LB agar + Kanamycin, and LB agar + Carbenicillin (or equivalent antibiotics used in the lab). The purpose of the plating is to check for contamination.

(2) Incubate plates and overnight culture  
Place plates inverted in a 37°C incubator. Incubate the 5 mL LB culture overnight (approximately 16 hours) in a 37°C shaking incubator.

(3) Prepare for Day 2 – Solutions and materials  
Prepare 400 mL LB medium in a 500 mL bottle. Prepare 30% glycerol solution by mixing 30 mL glycerol with 70 mL distilled H₂O. Prepare 0.1 M CaCl₂ by dissolving 1.47 g CaCl₂ in distilled H₂O and bringing the volume to 100 mL. Sterilize these (autoclave), label, and store at 4°C. Ensure you have at least 150 sterile 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes ready (factory-sterile or autoclaved). Autoclave a 500 mL Erlenmeyer flask (or equivalent for 200 mL culture) and seal with aluminum foil. Place four new, sealed, sterile 50 mL Falcon tubes in the 4°C fridge to chill (four tubes per 200 mL of final culture). Pre-chill aluminum beads at 4°C and –20°C gel packs/blocks to make sure you will have plenty of “ice” available for Day 2.

## Day 2 – Preparation of Competent Cells

### Equipment

- 500 mL Erlenmeyer flask (or equivalent for 200 mL culture)
- 37°C shaking incubator
- Spectrophotometer and cuvettes (for OD₆₀₀ measurements)
- Chilled 50 mL sterile Falcon tubes (four per 200 mL of culture)
- Refrigerated centrifuge with rotor for 50 mL tubes
- Chilled sterile 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes
- Storage box or rack for microcentrifuge tubes
- Ice bucket with chilled “ice” (gel packs, aluminum beads, etc.)

### Reagents

- Overnight *E. coli* BL21 (DE3) culture from Day 1
- LB broth (no antibiotics)
- Chilled, sterile 0.1 M CaCl₂ (4°C)
- Chilled, sterile 30% glycerol (4°C)
- Known plasmid for competency test (e.g., GFP plasmid)
- LB agar plates (no antibiotics)
- LB agar plates with appropriate antibiotics (e.g., Kanamycin, Carbenicillin)

### Procedure

(1) Grow culture to mid-log phase (OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.4–0.9)  
Turn on the centrifuge and set the temperature to 2°C; this can take up to 1 hour to reach. Add 200 mL LB broth (no antibiotics) to a 500 mL flask. Inoculate with 2 mL of the overnight culture from Day 1. Ensure sterility and seal the flask with foil. Place the flask in a 37°C shaking incubator (200–250 rpm). Measure OD₆₀₀ periodically using a spectrophotometer. Allow the culture to grow until OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.35; this typically takes about 2–3 hours. Once OD₆₀₀ is above 0.2, check every 15–20 minutes and avoid exceeding OD₆₀₀ = 0.9. Doubling time is approximately 20–30 minutes.

(2) Chill the culture  
When OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.35 is reached, place the entire flask in a 4°C refrigerator in a bucket of chilled “ice”. Chill for at least 10 minutes, ideally about 30 minutes, gently swirling occasionally to cool evenly. The target is to cool the culture below ~8°C to stop growth.

(3) Harvest cells  
Label four pre-chilled 50 mL sterile Falcon tubes. Distribute the chilled culture into these tubes (approximately 45 mL per tube). From this step onward, keep all tubes, reagents, and cells on ice whenever they are not in the chilled centrifuge. Centrifuge at 3500 rpm for 10 minutes at 2°C. Carefully pour off the supernatant without disturbing the cell pellet. If the pellet is loose, leave a small volume and remove the supernatant carefully with a pipette.

(4) First CaCl₂ wash  
Add 2.5 mL of ice-cold 0.1 M CaCl₂ to each pellet. Gently resuspend the cells in each tube by pipetting up and down or slowly swirling; avoid vigorous vortexing. Place the tubes on “ice” (and in the 4°C fridge as needed) and incubate for 30 minutes, keeping them colder than 8°C as much as possible (but above 0°C). Centrifuge again at 3500 rpm for 10 minutes at 2°C. Carefully remove and discard the supernatant; the pellet will be much looser now.

(5) Prepare competency mixture  
In a sterile, pre-chilled tube, mix 3 mL sterile 30% glycerol with 3 mL sterile 0.1 M CaCl₂ to make 6 mL total of competency mixture. Both reagents should be cold. Add 1.25 mL of this competency mixture to each cell pellet in the 50 mL tubes. Gently resuspend each pellet in the mixture until the suspension is uniform. Incubate the resuspended cells on ice for 30 minutes.

(6) Aliquot competent cells  
Optionally, pool the resuspended cells from all Falcon tubes into one pre-chilled sterile tube to make aliquoting easier, and keep this tube on ice. Label a storage box for pre-chilled 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes in advance with strain, date, and your initials to save time. Dispense 50 μL of the resuspended cells into each 1.5 mL tube while keeping both the master suspension and the tubes on ice. Expect this step to take up to about 1 hour; prepare extra ice so the cells remain cold the entire time.

(7) Freeze competent aliquots  
Confirm all remaining aliquots are stored with labels including strain, date, and your initials. Keep tubes on ice while organizing them into a storage box or rack. If possible, snap-freeze the tubes on dry ice or in a dry ice/ethanol bath before moving them to the –80°C freezer. If snap-freezing is not available, place them directly at –80°C while minimizing time off ice. Store long-term at –80°C and avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

(8) Test competency  
After freezing, use 3 aliquot tubes for a test transformation with a known plasmid (e.g., GFP plasmid). Perform a standard heat-shock transformation protocol for chemically competent *E. coli* (follow your lab’s specific timing and temperature steps). Plate transformed cells on LB (no antibiotics) to assess general viability and background growth, and on LB + Kanamycin and LB + Carbenicillin (or other appropriate antibiotic(s) for the plasmid used). Ensure plates are at room temperature before plating.
